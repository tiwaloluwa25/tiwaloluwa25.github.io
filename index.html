What the data says

    Rows are simulated for this portfolio: 7,043 customers across six regions. The headline figures, a 26.54% churn rate and $139,130 of monthly revenue at risk, match my project write-up. Amounts are in US dollars per month.

    The problem

    A subscription business was losing about one in four customers and could not say which ones were leaving or why. Retention offers were going out to everyone.

    What I did

    Built Power BI measures (DAX) for churn rate and monthly revenue lost, with Power Query to prepare the data, then cut churn by contract, tenure, support tickets and region. Checking the results in SQL exposed a DAX mistake that had inflated churn to 95.6%. I corrected it to 26.54%.

    What it changes

    Retention spend can focus on monthly-contract customers in their first six months and on anyone with repeated support tickets, instead of the whole base.

    What the data says

The headline figures, Neurology at about $143.6K of $657K total spending and a 3.07 satisfaction score against a 4.0 target, match my project write-up. Amounts are in US dollars.

      The problem

      Where is hospital spending concentrated, and how does patient experience compare with target? Managers wanted one view of cost, visit length, return visits and satisfaction to see which departments need attention first.

      What I did

      Used SQL to extract and clean the data, with Python (Pandas) for checks, then built the dashboard in Power BI and Looker Studio. Each department can be filtered by quarter to separate seasonal swings from lasting problems.

      What it changes

      Long waits show up directly in satisfaction scores, and Neurology stands out on both cost and return visits. The next step is to review Neurology staffing and add patient feedback to routine monitoring.

        Model comparison

        Three models trained on the same 4,000 borrowers. Best AUC in amber. Gradient boosting uses a 0.25 cut-off; the others use 0.50.

        ROC curve

        What drives default

        Relative weight in the logistic regression. Amber raises risk; green lowers it.

        Try the model: score a borrower

        The problem

        A lender wants to approve good borrowers quickly and avoid loans that will not be repaid. Checking each application by hand is slow and uneven.

        What I did

        Built a 5,000-row borrower dataset, split it into training and test sets, and compared three models in Python. I judged them on how many real defaulters they catch, not accuracy alone, because most borrowers repay and accuracy hides that.

        What it changes

        The cut-off is a business choice. A lower cut-off catches more defaulters but turns away more good borrowers, and the slider above shows that trade-off for each setting.
